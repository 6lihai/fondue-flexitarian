    What went down, and what went up?

    Less meat, still cheese. Meat eating peaked in 1987 and then fell. Milk is losing ground fast, but cheese keeps rising. And more people are cutting back on meat, even though few give it up completely.

    Who goes meat-free

    How many Swiss no longer eat meat?

    In 2024, 5.3% of people aged 14 to 74 were vegetarian (including 0.7% vegan). That's up from 2.9% in 2015. Young people and women lead the way, but recently men have been catching up.

    Source: Swissveg / MACH Consumer survey (about 30,000 people since 2020). Margin of error about ±0.6 points.

    So One person in 19 no longer eats meat. Young people and women lead the way.

    Why flexitarian?

    What is a flexitarian, and why now?

    Flexible + vegetarian. The word first appeared in a US newspaper in 1992. In 2003 the American Dialect Society voted it the most useful word of the year, and it entered the Merriam-Webster dictionary in 2012. Here is what it means, how many Swiss fit, and why it took off here.

    Industry, imports and exports

    Switzerland grows about half of what it eats

    Switzerland produces almost all its own milk and most of its meat. But it imports most plant foods, plus the animal feed that its livestock eat. Cheese and chocolate go out to the world, while more and more meat comes in.
